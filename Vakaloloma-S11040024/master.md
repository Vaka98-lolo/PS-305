# PS 305 Health Psychology - “Problem-Focused vs Emotion-Focused Coping: A Comparative Analysis of Stress and Coping Strategies”
## Reading 1:     
# Hart, Wearing, and Headey (1995) examined the personal and work-related factors influencing psychological well-being among police officers using a Perceived Quality of Life (PQOL) framework. The study involved 527 police officers and used a quantitative method with structural equation modelling to analyse questionnaire data. The findings showed that both positive and negative work experiences significantly affected well-being, with organizational experiences being more influential than operational factors. The results also indicated that personality traits such as neuroticism and extraversion were strong predictors of well-being. Importantly, problem-focused coping was associated with positive work experiences, while emotion-focused coping was linked to negative work experiences. The study concluded that coping strategies play a significant role in psychological well-being, supporting the effectiveness of problem-focused coping.
## Reading 2: 
# Park and Adler (2003) investigated how coping styles predict physical health and psychological well-being among first-year medical students. The study involved 71 students who were assessed at the beginning and end of the academic year using a longitudinal research design. The findings showed that students’ physical health and psychological well-being declined over time. However, students who used higher levels of problem-focused coping and approach-based emotion-focused coping experienced less deterioration in physical health. Psychological well-being was more strongly related to baseline functioning rather than coping style. The study concluded that coping strategies play a role in predicting physical health outcomes. This study is important as it shows that both problem-focused and certain types of emotion-focused coping can be beneficial in managing stress.
