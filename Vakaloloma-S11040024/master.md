# PS 305 Health Psychology - “Problem-Focused vs Emotion-Focused Coping: A Comparative Analysis of Stress and Coping Strategies”
## Reading 1:     
#Hart, Wearing, and Headey (1995) examined psychological well-being among police officers using a Perceived Quality of Life (PQOL) framework. The study involved 527 participants and applied a quantitative approach using structural equation modelling to analyse questionnaire data. The findings revealed that both positive and negative work experiences significantly influenced well-being, with organisational factors being more impactful than operational factors. Additionally, personality traits such as neuroticism and extraversion were strong predictors of psychological well-being. Coping strategies also played a key role, with problem-focused coping linked to positive work experiences, while emotion-focused coping was associated with negative outcomes.These findings can be better understood when viewed through an evolutionary perspective. Evolutionary theory suggests that human behaviours, including stress responses and coping mechanisms, developed as adaptive strategies to enhance survival. For example, problem-focused coping reflects an active response to environmental challenges, similar to survival behaviours that aim to eliminate threats. In contrast, emotion-focused coping helps regulate internal emotional states when external conditions cannot be immediately changed.
The evolutionary perspective is supported by research on human adaptation and behaviour. For instance, the development of human traits such as upright posture and behavioural flexibility reflects complex adaptive responses to environmental pressures rather than a single cause . Similarly, decision-making research shows that humans adjust their strategies depending on environmental uncertainty, demonstrating flexibility in behaviour . This aligns with the idea that coping strategies are not fixed but adapt based on situational demands.Therefore, the findings of Hart et al. (1995) support the view that psychological well-being is influenced by adaptive processes shaped through evolution. Effective coping strategies, particularly problem-focused coping, can be seen as evolved mechanisms that improve individuals’ ability to manage stress and maintain well-being in challenging environments.

## Reading 2: 
Park and Adler (2003) investigated how coping styles predict physical health and psychological well-being among first-year medical students. The study involved 71 students and used a longitudinal research design, assessing participants at the beginning and end of the academic year. The findings showed that both physical health and psychological well-being declined over time due to academic stress. However, students who used higher levels of problem-focused coping and approach-based emotion-focused coping experienced less deterioration in physical health. Psychological well-being was more strongly related to baseline functioning than coping style. The study concluded that coping strategies play an important role in predicting physical health outcomes, and that both problem-focused coping and certain forms of emotion-focused coping can be beneficial.

These findings can be understood through an evolutionary perspective, which suggests that coping mechanisms developed as adaptive responses to environmental stressors. Problem-focused coping reflects an active survival strategy aimed at removing or reducing threats, while approach-based emotion-focused coping helps regulate internal emotional states when stress cannot be immediately changed. This supports the idea that humans evolved flexible coping systems to manage both external challenges and internal emotional responses.

This is consistent with research on human adaptation, which shows that behaviour evolves in response to environmental pressures rather than a single cause . Similarly, decision-making research demonstrates that humans adjust their behaviour based on uncertainty and context, using adaptive but sometimes imperfect strategies . These findings suggest that coping strategies, like decision-making processes, are flexible and shaped by changing environments.

Furthermore, the role of stress in this study can be linked to evolutionary explanations of pain and discomfort as protective mechanisms. Stress, like pain, can act as a signal that something is wrong and requires attention, encouraging individuals to take action or adapt their behaviour. This explains why certain coping strategies help reduce the negative impact of stress on physical health.

Overall, Park and Adler’s (2003) study supports the view that coping strategies are adaptive mechanisms shaped by evolution. The effectiveness of both problem-focused and approach-based emotion-focused coping highlights the importance of flexibility in human behaviour, enabling individuals to respond effectively to different types of stressors and maintain health and well-being.
